# REVIEW.md

| # | Where (function / line) | What is wrong | How you'd notice it (test, input, or symptom) | How you fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `get_shift_context_for_datetime` / helper logic | The IST overnight-shift logic was incomplete and could assign the wrong attendance date when the punch time was before the overnight shift end. | A punch-in near midnight or an overnight shift could land on the wrong `date` and fail downstream filters. | Reworked the shift-context calculation to anchor the record date correctly for overnight shifts with a previous-day or next-day boundary check. |
| 2 | `compute_late_minutes` | The late window was effectively using a simplistic offset and did not enforce the contract’s “strictly more than 10 minutes” grace-period semantics in all cases. | Requests just over the grace boundary (e.g., 09:40:01) needed to compute 10 late minutes, while inside-grace times stayed at 0. | Kept the strict comparison and floor-based minute calculation aligned to the contract. |
| 3 | `compute_overtime_minutes` | Overtime was not correctly bound to the shift end and the 30-minute minimum threshold. | Punch-outs shortly after the shift end or under 30 minutes did not match expected totals. | Applied the `shift_end` delta logic with a `>= 30` threshold and floor semantics. |
| 4 | `compute_half_day` | Half-day logic needed to use the rounded `work_hours` value, not the raw duration. | A value like 4.49 or 4.50 had to behave as expected per the spec; raw floating-point comparisons were not reliable. | Converted to the rounded half-up value before comparing to 4.50. |
| 5 | `normalize_record_values` | Derived fields were not consistently produced for `ABSENT` / `LEAVE` and presence-status edge cases, risking invalid `late_minutes` / `overtime_minutes` values. | Some records could keep stale or incorrect values after a correction or a presence update. | Normalized every field explicitly so only valid presence statuses set derived values, and non-present statuses reset totals to zero/null defaults. |
| 6 | `serialize_record` / `serialize_employee` | Seeded JSON data uses MongoDB Extended JSON `{"$date": ...}` objects; the existing conversion assumed plain Python `datetime` objects. | Listing employees or attendance records against sample seed data raised an `AttributeError` when serializing. | Added coercion for BSON/Mongo Extended JSON date dicts before converting to epoch milliseconds. |
| 7 | `punch_in` / `punch_out` endpoints | The starter logic did not consistently validate the relationship between employee shift, record date, and punch timestamps. | Valid but tricky overnight or adjacent-day cases could produce the wrong date or an impossible punch-out. | Enforced the employee shift context and proper same-day / overnight constraints before accepting the event. |
| 8 | `regularize_attendance` | The original change flow could miss field-level diffs or fail to preserve history semantics for actual manual corrections. | Some edits could incorrectly skip recording history or accept a no-op payload. | Rebuilt the diff logic to compare old vs. new values, write only changed fields, and append exactly one audit history entry. |
| 9 | Aggregates in analytics endpoints | Some monthly and department calculations were using the wrong headcount or not counting employees with zero logs correctly. | Department summary or trend routes could undercount employees, especially when joining mid-month or lacking activity. | Switched to `joined_on <= period_end` logic and counted zero-log employees in the headcount calculations. |
| 10 | Leaderboard ranking | Tie handling in late-minute leaderboard required a shared rank for equal totals rather than a row-by-row sequential rank. | Two employees tied at the cutoff could get different ranks than the contract intended. | Implemented group-based ranking keyed by total late minutes, so identical totals share a rank. |
| 11 | Date and timestamp validation | Date strings were not consistently checked as real calendar dates, and Pydantic could coerce non-integer timestamp values. | Invalid attendance filters could return 200, while floats or strings could be accepted as epoch values. | Added strict calendar parsing at endpoint boundaries and strict integer/range validation for epoch-millisecond fields. |
| 12 | Department summary / trend | Departments with no logs were omitted, and trend rows were previously synthesized with a Python loop. | Zero-log employees were absent from summary output; trend execution did not meet the MongoDB gap-filling requirement. | Department names now come from the employee collection, and trend days, headcounts, rates, and moving averages are generated in an aggregation. |
| 13 | Overnight overtime | Overtime shift-end context was derived from the punch-out day, which misidentified the shift end after midnight. | An overnight punch-out after the scheduled morning end could incorrectly report no overtime. | Anchor the overtime shift window to the record's punch-in attendance date. |
| 14 | Monthly / department weekday analytics | Date-only strings were parsed at midnight IST, then `$dayOfWeek` evaluated the resulting instant in UTC, shifting the calendar date and misclassifying weekdays near midnight UTC. | Monday attendance could be excluded from `present_days`; the department trend rate could classify the same Monday incorrectly. | Evaluate `$dayOfWeek` explicitly in `Asia/Kolkata` in each monthly, department-summary, trend, and explain aggregation. |

Not a defect:
- The base use of `mongomock` / `MongoClient` with `tz_aware=True` was sound; the issue was not the database library, but the date coercion path when reading sample documents that arrived in Extended JSON form.
- The single-file architecture was not a problem; it matched the assignment requirement that all logic live in `app/main.py`.
- The use of unique indexes on `emp_code` and `(emp_code, date)` was correct and required for correctness under concurrent creates and duplicate punch-ins.

### Overnight lateness regression investigation

The reported `EMP99904` result was traced through the stored request instant, UTC/IST conversion, the selected employee shift, attendance date, and lateness calculation. The stored epoch-millisecond value `1791929700000` is `2026-10-13T22:15:00Z`, or `2026-10-14T03:45:00+05:30`; for the `22:00–06:00` shift, it belongs to the October 13 overnight shift and is 345 minutes after that shift's start. The value for the intended `2026-10-13T22:15:00+05:30` instant is `1791909900000`, which computes to 15 late minutes. `EMP99905`'s stored instant likewise decodes to `2026-10-13T00:42:09+05:30`, correctly assigned to its October 12 shift date. The reported values therefore came from different instants than the intended local wall times; no speculative shift-calculation change was made.

Automated regression tests now cover these timezone conversions and overnight boundaries. The API process serving port 8001 was traced to the project virtual-environment Uvicorn launcher, and its MongoDB explain namespace plus employee/log counts matched the direct local `attendance_db` inspection.
